## Signs of a moving landslide
- Sounds of cracking or breaking wood, knocking boulders, groaning ground, or other unusual noises, especially if they grow louder.
- Fast-moving landslides may produce a loud rumble and ground vibrations, similar to a passing freight train.
- Near a stream: a sudden change in water level, especially during or right after an intense or prolonged storm.

## If you notice these signs or observe a landslide in progress
- Leave the area immediately, but only if it is safe. Landslides can be sudden and can destroy a car or house instantly.
- If a landslide is hitting your building, don't evacuate. Go to the highest floor or climb onto a countertop until it passes; evacuating mid-slide can carry you away. Landslides may come in pulses; the first one may not be the last.
- Report the landslide immediately to local authorities.

## Signs of a potential landslide
- New cracks, bulges, or other deformation in the ground, roads, or other surfaces.
- Flowing water on a slope, or water ponding where it never has before.
- Sagging or tight utility lines, leaning utility poles, deformed fences, or trees tilting on a hillside.
- Sticking windows or doors, or new cracks in walls, ceilings, or foundations.
- Broken or leaking water, septic, or sewer lines.
- Structures separating from their foundations, or soil pulling away from a foundation.
- Abnormal changes in water well levels.

## If you observe warning signs
Report them to local authorities so the area can be evaluated. You may need to hire a professional geologist or engineer with landslide experience.

## Examples cited
- 10 Jan 2005, La Conchita (Ventura County, CA): 36 houses damaged/destroyed, 10 deaths.
- 3 Aug 2022: debris flow in the Hermits Peak–Calf Canyon burn area recorded by USGS camera.
